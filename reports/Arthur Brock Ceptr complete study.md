# Ceptr Prototyped the Stack Holochain Shipped

Ceptr is the decade-long attempt by Arthur Brock and Eric Harris-Braun to rebuild the computing and networking stack "from the receptive capacity" of living systems, and its complete body of work resolves into three unequal parts: a sprawling design vision (the *Ceptr Revelation*, a dozen whitepaper titles, nineteen blog posts, roughly a dozen recorded talks), a ~17,800-line C prototype written almost single-handedly by Harris-Braun between February 2013 and July 2016 that fully implements the intra-host half of the design (semantic trees, the Semtrex pattern language, receptors with expectations and signals, a tree-reducing VM with ~55 instructions, and pluggable protocols) while leaving the inter-host half (CeptrNet, the Compository, multi-instance "holographic" storage, membranes) on paper, and a single extracted component, the multi-instance synchronization layer, which was rewritten in Go in January 2017 as Holochain and went on to raise roughly 30,000 ETH and absorb the founders' careers. Ceptr grew directly out of the MetaCurrency Project's four "opens" (identity, rules, transport, data) after the project's XML-like rules format, XGFL, proved unable to make currencies interoperate, and its design principles (unenclosability, intrinsic data integrity, fractal sovereignty, "trust your own node") passed intact into Holochain even as semantic trees, Semtrex and the protocol-for-protocols did not. Ceptr proper has been dormant since mid-2016; the founders called it "shelved" (2018) and an "onramp" destination, official Holochain documents stopped mentioning it by 2024, and third-party reception never left the founders' own network. Then, on 22–23 February 2026, Harris-Braun un-broke the C build and pushed two new repositories, a 26,105-line Rust port (`ceptr-rs`) and a 4,486-line TypeScript port of semantic trees and Semtrex (`ceptr-js`), apparently as AI-assisted ports using the C code as ground truth, with the Rust port's own analysis admitting "system-level behavioral fidelity: LOW." The net picture is of a serious, coherent and still-unfinished research program whose most durable product was not the platform but the vocabulary and principles that Holochain carried into the world.

## MetaCurrency's failed rules format forced a rebuild of the stack

Ceptr's origin is a specific engineering failure inside a currency-design project. The MetaCurrency Project was publicly active by 2009, when the Institute for the Future announced a podcast describing it as "the brainchild of Brock (formerly of OS-Earth) and Eric Harris-Braun (of Open Money)" that "seeks to build platform and protocol standards that will allow multiple and interoperable currencies on the Internet" ([IFTF FutureCast, 16 Nov 2009](https://legacy.iftf.org/future-now/article-detail/futurecast-jerry-michalski-interviews-arthur-brock-on-alternative-currencies/)). Its stated requirements were four "opens": **Open Identity, Open Rules, Open Transport, Open Data**, with the framing that an open-source economy "requires new technology capacities which need to function in a non-monopolizable manner" ([P2P Foundation wiki: Metacurrency Project](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Metacurrency_Project)). Brock had arrived at this through earlier targeted-currency work in Denver (Geek Gene, Flowplace, Mile High Business Alliance), and his 2009 essays already contained Ceptr's later core idea, that data should carry its own integrity through git-like countersigned transaction chains rather than depend on firewalls ("We've replaced walls with laws and moats with tort") ([artbrock.com, The Footprints of Flow, 21 Mar 2009](https://www.artbrock.com/2009/03/21/the-footprints-of-flow)). Harris-Braun's contribution was the "flow mechanics" framing: eBay reputation, grades, coupons and airline miles "are all formal information systems that allow communities to interact with flows," and the project's goal was "a new expressive capacity" for them ([P2P Foundation wiki: Metacurrency Project](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Metacurrency_Project)).

The pivot from currency platform to computing stack happened at the Open Rules layer. The *Ceptr Revelation* explains that the team "initially implemented this with XGFL (eXtensible Game Format Language). You could drop an XGFL spec into a flowplace... We then discovered the issues of interoperability between rule-sets were much more complex than could be sensibly addressed by an expressive capacity akin to XML" ([Ceptr Revelation, Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Line362Wm0zMOZcEZMqPYfHqNS4XIVyVsP7SS_4jE2o/edit)). The same document reinterprets the four opens as one requirement, that the infrastructure be "unenclosable," and maps each layer onto a Ceptr mechanism: contextual name resolution and triangulated UUIDs for identity, executable self-describing protocols replacing XGFL for rules, a peer-to-peer CeptrNet with no central address authority for transport, and signed chains plus a DHT ("Intrinsic Data Integrity," adapted from Ian Grigg's triple-entry accounting) for data. By October 2011 a ~50-person MetaCurrency "Collabathon" was already describing a "NextNet" as "a computing/protocol stack for operating a distributed Internet" with a five-layer ladder (Stream-scapes, decision-making, currencies, Holoptinets, "OS Earth") and the aim of "nothing less than a self-describing computing" ([petervanstudios.com, 31 Oct 2011](http://petervanstudios.com/2011/10/31/metacurrency-collabathon-a-wealth-system/)). The Ceptr GitHub wiki states the lineage plainly: "Ceptr evolved from the intention of the MetaCurrency Project to built decentralized currencies for the next economy. It turned out, we needed more fundamental decentralization of our computing architectures," naming the prior iterations as openmoney, flowplace, commons room and anansi ([zippy/ceptr wiki](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/wiki)).

Dating the start requires triangulating several sources, because the founders later told podcast audiences that "Ceptr came out of [MetaCurrency] in 2006," a claim that refers to design ideas, not code ([Third Web #17 listing](https://podtail.com/podcast/the-third-web/the-third-web-17-arthur-brock-holochain/)). The best-supported chronology is: the Revelation Google Doc carries a "December 2010" date line under the authors' names; Brock wrote in November 2015 that the team had spent "the past 5 years" on Ceptr ([ceptr.org, 3 Nov 2015](https://ceptr.org/blog/2015-11-03-distributed-receptor-based-computing)); the Clojure predecessor `anansi`, "a reference implementation of a scaped receptor network" with host, user and scape receptor types and a Streamscapes communications app, has 373 of its 377 commits in 2011 ([github.com/zippy/anansi](https://github.com/zippy/anansi)); the C repository's first commit is 22 February 2013 ([github.com/zippy/ceptr](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr)); and the project only "came out of the MetaCurrency closet" publicly on 16 September 2014, when Brock admitted that "for the past few years" they had been pushing code to GitHub but "there's been no way for anyone to really understand what we're doing" ([ceptr.org, 16 Sep 2014](https://ceptr.org/blog/2014-09-16-hello-world)). The single most important inference from this record is that Ceptr was a private, two-person architecture effort for roughly four years before anyone outside the MetaCurrency circle saw it, and that this isolation, which Brock later diagnosed as "a communication problem," shaped everything about its reception.

## Receptive capacity and unenclosability define the design philosophy

The name is the thesis. Brock's November 2014 essay on the "receptive capacity breakthrough" argues that natural systems first receive a general carrier (sound, light) and only then pattern-match symbols, so that "receptive capacity comes before the symbols"; cochlear hairs respond to frequencies rather than words, eyes to light rather than objects, and atomic valences are "receptive slots" for electrons. A computer built this way "is designed to define and receive semantics, protocols, and carriers," and when it encounters an unknown symbol it can "install it in me so I can continue" rather than fail ([ceptr.org, 26 Nov 2014](https://ceptr.org/blog/2014-11-26-ceptr-design-receptive-capacity-breakthrough)). He explicitly linked this to "the subtle power of yin," and his GitHub bio still describes Ceptr as "a total rewrite of our computing/currency/comms stack from the feminine reCEPTive capacity using the organizational patterns we find in nature" ([github.com/artbrock](https://github.com/artbrock)). The same essay contains the engineering argument behind the biology: language layers (phonemes, words, phrases, narratives) each do independent sense-making, which is why listeners tolerate accents, whereas "if every layer depended strictly on base units, one error would corrupt everything above it," which is why most computer interfaces "break easily."

The *Ceptr Revelation* (subtitle: "Applying the patterns of living systems to upgrade social intelligence") turns this into six design principles: learn from nature; make it unenclosable (open and peered); foster coherence and follow convergence; intrinsic data integrity; fractal sovereignty (individuals can fork a group and keep their assets); and "optimized for evolution," with versioning and deprecation built into the lowest layer, summarized in the maxim "The model is: Trust your own node" ([ceptr.org/whitepapers/revelation](https://ceptr.org/whitepapers/revelation)). Its biological vocabulary is systematic rather than decorative: receptors are compared to senses and RNA; the carrier/signal/protocol pattern is said to recur "in physics, cells, speech, and TCP/IP"; "each cell carries the instructions for cooperation, with no 'CEO cell'"; the immune system models reputation-based filtering; and the whole is a "strange loop" in which each node is a genotype (a VMhost) existing at a phenotype address in the network. Brock's companion essay on Hofstadter makes the bootstrapping problem explicit: every receptor runs inside another receptor's address space, yet the network "exists/runs INSIDE the nodes, and yet the nodes exist INSIDE the network" ([ceptr.org, 14 Nov 2014](https://ceptr.org/blog/2014-11-14-i-am-a-strange-loop-ceptr-too)).

Underneath the biology sits a theory of history. The "Grammatic Capacities" draft (Harris-Braun and Brock) argues that new complex adaptive systems "always appear concomitant with the emergence of a special type of informational infrastructure," a grammatic capacity, stacking from phonemes to words and from clay tokens to writing, printing and networked computing, and concludes that what is needed now is "a Grammatic Capacity with the ability to express Grammatic Capacities, i.e. a Protocol for Protocols" ([ceptr.org/whitepapers/grammatics](https://ceptr.org/whitepapers/grammatics)). Harris-Braun had already told the same parable in 2010, temple scribes versus the alphabet, concluding that "a wealth alphabet" must precede economic democracy as literacy preceded political democracy ([eric.harris-braun.com, 8 Sep 2010](https://eric.harris-braun.com/blog/2010/09/08/a-story-about-expressive-capacity/)). Brock's version is "social DNA": corporations are "literal living organisms," currencies are their DNA, and "we labor under the illusion that changing the people changes the organism" ([artbrock.com, 26 Apr 2009](https://www.artbrock.com/2009/04/26/currencies-as-dna-of-social-organisms)). Ceptr is the medium in which such DNA could be written and run: "Like DNA in cells, each user of that currency runs their own instance of that receptor" ([Ceptr Revelation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Line362Wm0zMOZcEZMqPYfHqNS4XIVyVsP7SS_4jE2o/edit)). This is why Brock insisted Ceptr could not be assembled from existing tools: "I know that we won't get what we want from cobbling together a mish-mash of other tools (like GO, node.js, haskell, etherium, Diaspora, IFTT, RDF, XDI, DPKI, IPFS and ErisDB)," because none of them run "from router firmware up to operating systems" ([artbrock.com, 17 Nov 2015](https://www.artbrock.com/2015/11/17/why-you-havent-heard-of-ceptr-metacurrency-has-been-underground)). The 2015 MIT webinar deck put the ambition on one slide: "Way beyond block-chain, APIs or RDF!" ([Prezi: Just a Spoonful of Ceptr](https://prezi.com/y_7109dt0gzj/just-a-spoonful-of-ceptr/)).

## Semantic trees, Semtrex and receptors form one homoiconic substrate

The technical architecture is best understood from the C code, which is the only place the design is fully specified; every ceptr.org design page is marked "Concept" or "Pre-release Draft." The pieces fit together as one substrate: everything in memory is a semantic tree; Semtrex matches trees; receptors hold trees and react to incoming trees via Semtrex-based expectations; protocols are trees of expectations with abstract slots; processes are trees that reduce themselves; and the VM host schedules receptors and routes signal trees between them.

### Semantic trees replace both types and wire formats

"Semantic trees are the basic data building block for ceptr. Everything in ceptr is built out of semantic trees. In ceptr all data is assumed to be meaningfull, not just structured" ([tree.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/tree.h)). A node pairs a `Symbol` (what the data means, such as age or shoe size) with a raw surface whose layout is given by the Symbol's declared `Structure` (how bits are organized, such as INTEGER). The identifier is a three-part `SemanticID {Context context /*uint32*/; SemanticType semtype /*uint16*/; SemanticAddr id /*uint16*/}` with five semantic types (STRUCTURE, SYMBOL, PROCESS, RECEPTOR, PROTOCOL), yielding the comment "4G types of receptors" and "64K types of symbols/structure per receptor" ([ceptr_types.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/ceptr_types.h)). The defining move is **semantic alternation**: a Structure is itself a composition of Symbols, so definitions recurse (`Symbol: YEAR,INTEGER; Structure: DATE,YEAR,MONTH,DAY; Symbol: TODAY,DATE; Structure: TIMESTAMP,TODAY,NOW; Symbol: TICK,TIMESTAMP`) down to a small set of "bottoming-out" structures (BIT, INTEGER, FLOAT, CHAR, CSTRING, UUID, BLOB, TREE and a few others) that are defined with `NULL_SYMBOL` "because it's not really defined in ceptr terms" ([base_defs](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/base_defs)). Brock named the pattern in 2014 using the example Home_Location → LatLong → Latitude/Longitude floats → mantissa/exponent → bits → flip-flops ([ceptr.org, 29 Nov 2014](https://ceptr.org/blog/2014-11-29-building-meaning-through-semantic-alternation)), and the webinar slogan was "you can't instantiate meaningless data... you can't store an int, you can store an age or a shoe size" ([MIT/KIT webinar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)).

Because meaning and structure travel with the data, Ceptr claimed to dispense with both the compile-time type system and the separate wire format: "Unlike XML or JSON text labels, the page uses semantic IDs that point to a complete semantic definition covering data type, structure, and special operators" ([ceptr.org/projects/pcubed](http://ceptr.org/projects/pcubed)), and "The fundamental data unit in Ceptr is not a 32 or 64 bit word; it's a semantic tree... Ceptr programs are just another type of semantic tree" ([ceptr.org/projects/core](http://ceptr.org/projects/core)). In code this cashes out as runtime type-checking by pattern matching: `_d_build_def_semtrex()` compiles a Symbol's structure definition into a Semtrex and `_r_def_match()` validates an instance against it ([receptor.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/receptor.h)). Definitions themselves are semantic trees (`DEFINITIONS = (STRUCTURES, SYMBOLS, PROCESSES, PROTOCOLS, RECEPTORS, SCAPES)`), label tables map human-readable strings in multiple languages onto symbols, and a lisp-like text form `(SYMBOL:value)` / `(SYMBOL (child) ...)` is parsed by `_t_parse()` and printed by `_t2s()`. The 16-bit address space and the `MAX_CONTEXTS 100` limit are prototype limits, not design limits; the design docs speak of UUID-sized identifiers everywhere.

### Semtrex is a tree automaton with named-capture embodiment

Semtrex ("SEMantic Tree Regular EXpressions") "provides a language for pattern matching on semantic trees similar to regular expressions for matching on text strings," and "a Semtrex is itself a semantic tree" with a linear textual syntax ([doxy/semtrex.dox](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/doxy/semtrex.dox)). The grammar has a root `/`, sequence `,`, sibling groups `( )`, alternation `|`, quantifiers `+ * ?`, a walk operator `%` ("find anywhere in the tree"), wildcard `.`, negation `!`, value literals (`SYM=val`, sets `SYM={a,b}`), and captures `<SYM:...>`. The engine is explicitly built on Russ Cox's regex-to-NFA work (`cox2007.pdf` ships in the repo), with the twist that transitions are tree moves rather than "next character": "In an old fashioned regex, the transition is implicit because it's always 'NextCharacter'. For semtrex we need to expand the possibilities," so transitions are next-sibling, descend, or "pop up that many levels plus advance to next child" ([semtrex.c](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/semtrex.c)). Matching returns a result tree of `SEMTREX_MATCH` nodes with symbol, path and sibling count, and `_t_embody_from_match()` builds a new semantic tree from the captures, so that parsing and typing happen in one step: the capture symbol becomes the Symbol of the embodied node.

The worked example from the co-authored "Introducing Semtrex" article turns the string `42.25,73.25` into an ASCII tree, matches `/ASCII_CHARS/<HomeLocation:<lat:ASCII_CHAR+>,ASCII_CHAR=',',<lon:ASCII_CHAR+>>`, embodies a `HomeLocation` tree with float `lat` and `lon`, and then matches `/%HomeLocation/(lat=42.25,lon=73.25)` against it; "Semtrex matches on the semantics of the data as well as literal values, whereas Regex, having no concept of the semantics on which it is matching, can match only on the literal values" ([Semtrex Intro article in repo](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/articles/Semtrex%20Intro/semtrex%20intro.htm); [CodeProject: Introducing Semtrex](https://www.codeproject.com/Articles/894188/Introducing-Semtrex)). The most ambitious single expression in the codebase parses an entire HTTP request line out of a character tree, capturing method, path segments, query parameters and version in one Semtrex ([http_example.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/spec/http_example.h)). Semtrex thus plays three roles that the design documents' phrase "universal parser" conflates: structural validation of instances, expectation matching inside receptors, and edge parsing of byte streams into semantic trees. Even the Semtrex text parser uses Semtrex on its own token tree. Only `SEMTREX_NOT` (`~`) is declared but marked "not implemented."

### Receptors, aspects, expectations and signals

The design says "A Receptor is a lightweight virtual machine. Receptors can contain other receptors, which is what enables the fractal layers of computing," and receptors "have membranes, are organized fractally, receive and send signals over bound carriers" ([doxy/main.dox](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/doxy/main.dox); [Prezi](https://prezi.com/y_7109dt0gzj/just-a-spoonful-of-ceptr/)). In code, a receptor is a `RECEPTOR_INSTANCE` tree mirrored by a C struct holding its own semantic context (definitions), an instance store addressed by `Xaddr {Symbol symbol; int addr}`, a "flux" of aspects each holding `EXPECTATIONS` and `SIGNALS`, pending outbound signals and responses, conversations, and a process queue ([ceptr_types.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/ceptr_types.h)). An expectation is the tuple `EXPECTATION = (CARRIER, PATTERN, ACTION, PARAMS, END_CONDITIONS, ?SEMANTIC_MAP, ?CONVERSATION_UUID)`, where the pattern is a Semtrex and end conditions are a timeout or repetition count; the webinar's Prolog-like reading was "ON carrier EXPECT semtrex_pattern DO action" ([base_defs](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/base_defs)). A signal is `SIGNAL = (ENVELOPE(SIGNAL_UUID), MESSAGE(HEAD, BODY))` with `HEAD = (FROM_ADDRESS, TO_ADDRESS, ASPECT_IDENT, CARRIER, ?CONVERSATION_IDENT, ?(END_CONDITIONS | IN_RESPONSE_TO_UUID))`. Delivery (`_r_deliver`) appends the signal to the aspect's SIGNALS and tests every expectation on that aspect: carrier must match, the pattern is matched against the body, end conditions are decremented, and on a match the action's run tree is built with parameters filled from the match and queued ([receptor.c](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/receptor.c)). Harris-Braun's 2016 dev post documents the signaling instruction set that exposes this to Ceptr code, SAY, REQUEST, RESPOND, LISTEN, CONVERSE, COMPLETE, THIS_SCOPE and SELF_ADDR, with synchronous and asynchronous variants ([ceptr.org, 27 Apr 2016](https://ceptr.org/blog/2016-04-27-musings-on-coding-in-ceptr-signaling)).

The gap between design and code is visible here. The only trace of a two-sided membrane is an `enum AspectType {EXTERNAL_ASPECT, INTERNAL_ASPECT}` with the comment "aspects appear on either side of the membrane"; receptor nesting is implemented only as definition contexts chained by parent context plus a flat per-host routing table of 1,000 integer addresses, with no runtime containment of child receptors ([vmhost.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/vmhost.h)). "Carrier" in the implementation is simply a Symbol in the HEAD that expectations filter on before Semtrex runs; the hormone/lock-and-key language is conceptual.

### Pluggable protocols are late-bound templates

The Pcubed page states the thesis: a protocol is "live, executable, component, not an RFC paper," and "what is typically called a 'protocol' in a specification is for us an EMPTY protocol template whose connection points must be bound when it is installed" ([ceptr.org/projects/pcubed](http://ceptr.org/projects/pcubed)). The code realizes this precisely. `PROTOCOL_DEFINITION = (PROTOCOL_LABEL, PROTOCOL_SEMANTICS, *PROTOCOL_DEFAULTS, *INTERACTION, *INCLUSION)`, where `PROTOCOL_SEMANTICS = (*ROLE, *GOAL, *USAGE, *WEAL)` are abstract references ("A role stands for an expected agent," "A goal stands for an intended process," "A usage stands for a(n) X of a Symbol," "A weal stands for a included protocol"), and each interaction is a set of `EXPECT (ROLE, SOURCE, PATTERN, ACTION)` and `INITIATE (ROLE, DESTINATION, ACTION)` clauses ([base_defs](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/base_defs)). "Pluggable" means binding those slots at express time: `_o_express_role(receptor, protocol, role, aspect, bindings)` unwraps the definition, resolves slots from `PROTOCOL_BINDINGS` (RESOLUTIONs mapping GOAL→ACTUAL_PROCESS, ROLE→ACTUAL_RECEPTOR, USAGE→ACTUAL_SYMBOL) and installs the role's EXPECTs as expectations on an aspect, raising "binding missing for GOAL" if anything is left abstract ([protocol.c](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/protocol.c)). "Wrapping" is `INCLUSION`: the RECOGNIZE protocol ("like ping, only semantic") includes the abstract REQUESTING protocol, LINKs REQUESTER→RECOGNIZER and RESPONDER→RECOGNIZEE, and RESOLVEs the request type to `are_you`, the response to `i_am` and the handler to `fill_i_am`; `_o_unwrap()` macro-expands this into a concrete protocol plus a semantic map carried into every expectation ([protocol_spec.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/spec/protocol_spec.h)). The HTTP protocol in `contexts/INTERNET.cptr` is built the same way, as an INCLUSION of REQUESTING with HTTP_CLIENT/HTTP_SERVER roles and HTTP_REQUEST/HTTP_RESPONSE symbols on an HTTP_ASPECT ([INTERNET.cptr](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/contexts/INTERNET.cptr)). The webinar's rhetorical contrast was that "the HTTP 1.1 specification is almost 300 pages long of text... it's not something that runs," whereas in Ceptr HTTP "is pretty simple, there's just server and client and there's only two types of messages" ([MIT/KIT webinar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)). What was not built: the "transformational maps" (direct, combined, computed, dropped, merged) that the Pcubed page describes ("The map transformer is not yet built"), SMTP/FTP front-end protocols, and the Revelation's protocol negotiation in which a receptor installs an unknown protocol and continues.

### Scapes shrank from relational landscape to hash index

On paper, scapes are Ceptr's answer to tables and RDF: they "manage collections of data elements and encode the relationships and dimensions of variability between them," with geometries such as sequence, list, map, tree, set membership and lat/long, and a proposed but unspecified "scape algebra" of crossing, intersecting and blending ([ceptr.org/whitepapers/revelation](http://ceptr.org/whitepapers/revelation)). Harris-Braun's "Bicameral Brain" post made scapes one of the two loci of gestalt pattern matching (alongside membranes), "a kind of data landscape" in which listeners fire as data changes ("your friend is nearby"), and proposed offloading Semtrex matching to GPUs ([ceptr.org, 9 Jul 2016](https://ceptr.org/blog/2016-07-09-ceptr-and-the-bicameral-brain)). In code, `scape.c` is 93 lines: `Scape {Symbol key_source; Symbol data_source; hash(key-tree) → Xaddr}` with `_s_new/_s_add/_s_get`, used by the VMHost to avoid re-installing a receptor package ([scape.c](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/scape.c)). No scape query instruction exists in the VM.

### The VM host reduces self-eating run trees

The VMHost is a per-machine process that owns the semantic table, a routing table, an active-receptor list and a main thread that round-robins over active receptors, reducing each one's process queue and then delivering its pending signals; the code comments "Really this should be a thread pool manager" ([vmhost.c](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/vmhost.c)). Execution is tree rewriting: a process is instantiated as `RUN_TREE = (CODE, PARAMS, ?error-CODE, ?error-PARAMS)`, "think about this as a stack frame," and a stepper walks it through Descend/Ascend/Eval/Pushed/Pop/Block states, replacing `PARAM_REF` and `SIGNAL_REF` path nodes with values, reducing system instructions in place and pushing a new context for user-defined processes ([process.c](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/process.c)). Harris-Braun's phrase was "a self-eating tree that reduces itself to its result" replacing the call stack ([ceptr.org, 6 Apr 2015](https://ceptr.org/blog/2015-04-06-semantic-trees)). The implemented instruction set (the case labels of `__p_reduce_sys_proc`) comprises definition ops (DEF_SYMBOL, DEF_STRUCTURE, DEF_PROCESS, DEF_RECEPTOR, DEF_PROTOCOL), instance ops (NEW, GET, DEL), control flow (COND, IF, ITERATE, DO, RAISE, CONTINUE), signaling (SAY, REQUEST, RESPOND, LISTEN, CONVERSE, COMPLETE, INITIATE_PROTOCOL), matching and templating (MATCH, FILL, FILL_FROM_MATCH, TRANSCODE), stream I/O (STREAM_READ/WRITE/ALIVE/CLOSE), string and integer arithmetic, and a frank `MAGIC` instruction that "reduces to hardcoded special things as a scaffold for things we don't know how to build in ceptr yet." Blocking primitives (REQUEST without callback, LISTEN without action, STREAM_READ) move a context to a blocked queue and wake it via a `WAKEUP_REFERENCE`, which is the concrete content of the slogan "parallel tree processing."

The running node that `main()` composes is modest but real: a clock receptor ticking once a second, a shell receptor wired to stdin/stdout via stream-edge receptors with four commands (`time`, `receptors`, `debug`, `quit`), and an HTTP listener on port 8888 whose connection handler runs a CONVERSE/ITERATE loop that STREAM_READs a request and initiates the HTTP protocol ([ceptr.c](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/ceptr.c)). The 2026 Rust port's analysis confirms that even the `time` command flows through a genuine multi-receptor, signal-mediated pipeline (input edge → shell → clock → response handler) ([ceptr-rs PORT-ANALYSIS.md](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr-rs/blob/main/PORT-ANALYSIS.md)). A file-based "Accumulator" ("the Accumulator takes care of persistence and signaling... the 'body' of receptors") exists but its boot call is commented out in `main()` ([accumulator.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/src/accumulator.h)).

## 17,800 lines of C built the intra-host half and left the network on paper

The canonical repository `zippy/ceptr` (GPLv3, "Copyright (C) 2013-2016, The MetaCurrency Project (Eric Harris-Braun, Arthur Brock, et. al.)") holds **990 commits**, distributed 96 in 2013, 371 in 2014, 340 in 2015, 177 in 2016, and then one or two a year ([github.com/zippy/ceptr](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr)). Of 966 non-merge commits, **Eric Harris-Braun wrote 846 (87.6 percent)**; Marc Clifton contributed 70 (a Windows C# IDE and build notes, not the runtime), Lewis Hoffman 13 plus 26 pair-programmed commits in late 2013, and Arthur Brock 8, all README and documentation edits ([contributors](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/graphs/contributors)). Substantive development stopped in **July 2016** (last code commits: "bug fixes to semtrex #155" and label-table fixes), with the Doxygen site last regenerated on 5 August 2016 ([zippy.github.io/ceptr](http://zippy.github.io/ceptr/)). The `src/` directory holds 17,787 lines across 42 files, dominated by `process.c` (2,271 lines), `semtrex.c` (2,149), `tree.c` (2,092), `receptor.c` (1,303) and the auto-generated `base_defs.c/.h`; the system-definitions DSL `base_defs` declares 7 contexts, 54 structures, 430 symbols, 59 processes and 7 protocols. A `fresh_start` tag on 11 June 2014 marks the restart of the current tree after an earlier C "periodic_table" design (24 headers, "runs clean, 31 passing" in January 2014) was moved to `archive/` alongside C++ and Clojure sketches; there was never a Ruby, Python or CoffeeScript Ceptr, and no package was ever published to npm, RubyGems or crates.io.

The test suite is the best evidence of what works. `make test` builds a single executable from 17 `spec/*_spec.h` headers (7,672 lines) with a 43-line macro framework; statically there are **182 test functions and 1,098 assertions**, led by `process_spec.h` (297 assertions), `tree_spec.h` (157), `semtrex_spec.h` (140), `receptor_spec.h` (115) and `mtree_spec.h` (104) ([spec dir](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/tree/master/spec)). `testProtocolRecognize` exercises the full cycle, fetching RECOGNIZE, unwrapping it, expressing both roles, initiating, reducing the queue, delivering signals, and asserting the exact reply `(i_am (RECEPTOR_LABEL (ENGLISH_LABEL:super cept)) (RECEPTOR_IDENTIFIER:314159))` ([protocol_spec.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/spec/protocol_spec.h)). Specs double as documentation, pulled into the Doxygen API via `@snippet`, and emit JSON tree dumps for a D3 visualizer "used for creating visuals for presentation purposes" ([README](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/README.md)). The README's "Usage" section reads "TBD."

The table below separates what the Revelation and the 2015–16 decks promised from what the repository contains.

The pattern is unmistakable: everything that lives inside one machine was built and tested, and everything between machines, which is also everything the 2015 Rebooting-the-Web-of-Trust brief listed as working ("configurable Byzantine fault tolerance with signed chains," "permanent UUID identity with public keys in a sharded DHT") ([ceptr.org, 3 Nov 2015](https://ceptr.org/blog/2015-11-03-distributed-receptor-based-computing)), existed as prose. The `vmhost_spec.h` file contains a fully commented-out test that would have installed an "http server" receptor package and a "hello world app" bound to helloworld.com, which is the closest thing to the end-to-end demo the project kept promising ([vmhost_spec.h](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/blob/master/spec/vmhost_spec.h)). The brief's claim list should therefore be read as a description of the design, not the artifact, a distinction the founders themselves did not always draw for audiences.

## Nineteen posts, three real whitepapers and a dozen recordings make up the corpus

The written Ceptr corpus is smaller than its twelve-title whitepaper index suggests. The ceptr.org blog holds exactly **19 posts** between 16 September 2014 and 12 June 2017, 13 by Brock (vision, events, essays) and 6 by Harris-Braun (the 2016 "Musings on Coding in Ceptr" dev series, the restructuring and Holochain sprint posts) ([ceptr.org/blog](https://ceptr.org/blog/)). Of the twelve whitepaper entries, only three have substantial bodies: the *Ceptr Revelation* (a living Google Doc started December 2010, first released September 2014, still containing sections marked "Still to write/expand," and an appendix comparing Ceptr to Bitcoin, Ethereum, Diaspora, MaidSafe, Synereo, IPFS, RINA, XDI/XRI, OpenID and others); "Grammatic Capacities and the Evolution of Complex Adaptive Systems" (a pre-release draft with bracketed unfinished notes such as "[DNA as a grammatic capacity for creating Agents!]"); and "Mutual Credit Cryptocurrencies: Beyond Blockchain Bottlenecks" (dated 31 March 2016, the republication of the Medium essay "Beyond Blockchain: Simple Scalable Cryptocurrencies") ([ceptr.org/whitepapers](https://ceptr.org/whitepapers/)). The Pcubed, Fractal Virtual Machine, Holoptinets, Network Intelligence, Sovereign Accountable Commons and Fallacy of Individualism papers are title-and-status stubs with no body text. The January 2017 Jekyll rebuild added six project pages (Pcubed, Holochain, Holo, Sovereign Commons, Neural Internetworking, Ceptr Core) and six "participate" pages (residencies, opportunities, hackathons, explorations, operations, salons), which together are the fullest prose statement of the architecture that exists ([ceptr.org/projects/core](https://ceptr.org/projects/core)). Around this sits a halo of Google Docs ("A Different Approach to Cryptocurrencies," the "Ceptr Fuel Currency" design that is the direct ancestor of HoloFuel, a 2016 "Protocols for Inclusion and Exclusion" for the dev community), the Doxygen API docs, the GitHub wikis, the 2011 anansi READMEs, and some thirty artbrock.com essays from 2009 to 2017 that supply the conceptual program.

The recorded material is similarly concentrated. The Ceptr-era core (2014–2016) comprises the 15 September 2014 Hangout On Air (recording not located; its Prezi survives), the 1 April 2015 dress rehearsal and the **2 April 2015 MIT Kerberos and Internet Trust webinar** (Brock, Harris-Braun, Marc Clifton; 1:01:22; 4,831 views) with its two short demo videos on semantic trees and run-tree processing ([YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)), Brock's written brief for Rebooting the Web of Trust in November 2015, Jean-François Noubel's presentation of the framework at the Global Brain Institute in Brussels on 4 December 2015, and the **three-hour "Ceptr Technology: Under the Hood" session at Impact Hub Oakland on 25 January 2016** (4,923 views; no captions, so its content is known mainly through the "Ceptr Tech Overview" Prezi) ([YouTube Y9ZhswdOm14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y9ZhswdOm14); [Prezi raptqxuputwp](https://prezi.com/raptqxuputwp/ceptr-tech-overview/)). No conference-circuit Ceptr talk at IIW, DWeb Summit or any blockchain conference was found; the team used Prezi exclusively and self-published everything on the MetaCurrency Project YouTube channel. The 2017 cluster ("Ceptr Salons," "Potlucks," the Alchemical Universe residency videos, the July 2017 "Why are we building Holochain" conversation) documents the hand-off, and the 2018 cluster (CryptoTickerDE, Plutopia, Denver hackathon, Future Fossils, Crypto Radio) describes Ceptr retrospectively. The demos actually shown were deliberately low-level: Clifton's HOPE environment (a C# receptor/membrane system that "isn't actually built on Ceptr") scraping NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day, the lat/long semantic tree, a mailing-label run tree rendered in D3 "like a hex dump of the running code," a Semtrex HTTP-path match, and HTTP-as-two-messages ([MIT/KIT webinar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)). Brock conceded that Ceptr "still doesn't exactly have like a user interface" ([dress rehearsal, 1 Apr 2015](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)).

Brock tuned the pitch by audience while keeping the same analogies. Developers heard "Ceptr is really the underlying low-level protocols for how to structure data, organize processing and communicate as well as share code," built "in C for speed and portability... potentially being embedded in things like wi-fi routers" ([MIT/KIT webinar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)). Currency practitioners heard that "a college degree is a reputation currency... grades are performance metric currency," that "all of those data packages live in large silos," and that "Ceptr is our answer to that, it's the interoperable grammatic space." Crypto audiences in 2018 heard "proof of work and proof of stake are both centralization algorithms" and that Ceptr "will win with its self-describing protocols because of the information fragmentation problem" ([CryptoTickerDE, Feb 2018](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8ta-561BSE)); hackathon developers were told Ceptr is "crazy and weird and requires you to bend your brains in painful ways" ([Denver hackathon, Jul 2018](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wBlwQpcV95M)).

The application proposals, consistently ordered currencies → communications → organizations → sensing, were general infrastructure claims with concrete targets. The founding use case was interoperable currencies and reputation: "In Ceptr, each participant in a currency runs a copy of that currency's receptor" ([A Different Approach to Cryptocurrencies](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xX5yoqegfLNw3__VSOPDdiayibXlE2XGLDapg-Bi3tc/edit)), and a user joining a currency would "grab a copy of that currency's receptor current version from the compository, install it, create your identity" ([dress rehearsal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)). Streamscapes, prototyped in Clojure in 2011, was to unify RSS, email, Twitter, IM, Skype and CRM "into one landscape" ([anansi README-streamscapes](https://github.com/zippy/anansi/blob/master/README-streamscapes.markdown)). Sovereign Accountable Commons were "organizations incorporated in distributed app code instead of legal codes," explicitly "Ceptr's parallel to the Ethereum DAO" but with forkable "mutual sovereignty" ([ceptr.org/projects/sovereign](https://ceptr.org/projects/sovereign)). Neural Internetworking proposed "sticky requests" such as planting a listener on a remote weather station for a 2.5-millibar pressure drop ([ceptr.org/projects/neural](https://ceptr.org/projects/neural)). The Compository was to be "a built-in, fully-distributed github" with reputation-based review and a "Ceptr Fuel" mutual-credit currency redeemable for hosting ([Ceptr Fuel doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1zchqaIR1QPOFUVBXd4ryamwyrNVRE0VztC62pOho6Rw/edit)). Ceptr Core imagined "flashing your WiFi router with some Ceptr firmware" for mesh networking against an "Internet Kill Switch" ([ceptr.org/projects/core](https://ceptr.org/projects/core)). Every one of these shares the pattern "each participant runs their own receptor and chain; shared data in a DHT; agreements in code," which is exactly why the list reappeared nearly unchanged as Holochain's 2017 app ideas (distributed Twitter "Clutter," Slack-alike "Holochat," Trust Exchange, mutual-credit currencies).

## Blockchain timing and first funding carved Holochain out in January 2017

The transition can be dated to the week. The `holochain-proto` repository's first commit is Arthur Brock's "Initial commit" on **23 December 2016**, followed by Harris-Braun's "code sprint for initial command line holochain peer" on 4 January 2017 ([github.com/holochain/holochain-proto](https://github.com/holochain/holochain-proto)). On 17 January 2017 Harris-Braun announced that "we've broken the Ceptr development effort into some smaller sub-projects that are each independently valuable" to "increase surface area for participation": **holochain** ("provides the distributed data-store for Ceptr... on the drawing board for quite a while"), **Pcubed** (semantic trees and Semtrex extracted as a library) and **ceptr-core** (binding both to the VM host as "our fractal receptor compute fabric"), adding "I've jumped to golang for holochain development" ([ceptr.org, 17 Jan 2017](https://ceptr.org/blog/2017-01-17-restructuring-ceptr-sub-projects)). By 21 February the source hash-chain and "Nucleus" were working and the DHT was being built on IPFS's libp2p; the first proof-of-concept hackathon ran in San Francisco on 6–8 March 2017 ([ceptr.org, 21 Feb 2017](https://ceptr.org/blog/2017-02-21-holochain_dev_sprint)). Only Holochain was ever shipped; the `metacurrency/Pcubed` repository, created in December 2016, is 14 KB of nothing, and the Go rewrite of ceptr-core never began.

The founders gave two reasons in their own words. The first was market timing: Brock wrote in June 2017 that "Blockchain has become quite the rage... Global consensus on local independent actions... will never scale. If we don't provide a better alternative before people sour on blockchain limitations, people may not be willing to listen" ([ceptr.org, 12 Jun 2017](https://ceptr.org/blog/2017-06-12-phase-shift)), and told the Denver hackathon a year later that "we specifically spun off Holochain to plot an intercept course with blockchain to try to Aikido all of that enthusiasm, energy, resources, attention on to a more sustainable track" ([Denver hackathon, Jul 2018](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wBlwQpcV95M)). The second was money: "we must navigate the transition from a tech hobby project to a scalable business, in part, because we've been approached by funders... We just got our initial funds in the door in last weeks," via Village Lab's Wealth Stewardship Circle model, with "a secret team... to explore options related to doing an ICO" and a team that had doubled from 6 to 12 ([ceptr.org, 12 Jun 2017](https://ceptr.org/blog/2017-06-12-phase-shift)). Before that point no Kickstarter, grant or crowdfunding for Ceptr is documented anywhere; the site said in January 2017 that Ceptr was "funded only by the sweat of our team." A third, implicit reason is the 2015 "communication problem": Brock had written that "what we're building is so big and broad, that most people don't make it far enough through the story," and that critics responded "'RDF does that,' or 'Sounds like the blockchain to me,' or 'Can't you just mash together Diaspora, XDI and IPFS'" ([artbrock.com, 17 Nov 2015](https://www.artbrock.com/2015/11/17/why-you-havent-heard-of-ceptr-metacurrency-has-been-underground)). Holochain was a story people could follow. The outcome Brock reported in 2019 was an "Initial Community Offering" of roughly 30,000 ETH (about $20 million) and a Rust rebuild with its own missteps ([artbrock.com, 21 May 2019](https://www.artbrock.com/2019/05/21/holochain-wins-missteps-and-next-steps)).

What crossed over was the data-integrity slice and the principles; what stayed behind was the semantic substrate. The Ceptr README (updated by Brock in August 2017) states: "We pulled out a small part of Ceptr (synchronization for multi-instance receptors) and are going head-to-head with blockchain... That portion of Ceptr is called Holochain," adding that "we may scrap this codebase in C and rebuild it Go. Holochain is getting a lot more traction in Go, than Ceptr got written in C" ([github.com/zippy/ceptr](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr)). Brock's 2016 "Perspectives on Blockchains" principles, Intrinsic Data Integrity, Distributed Process not Consensus, Agents not Coins, Fractal not Global ("Systems will never scale if you require global consensus for local actions by independent agents"), became Holochain's identity ([P2P Foundation wiki: Ceptr](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Ceptr)). The DNA metaphor was already Revelation vocabulary before it became Holochain's "DNA" terminology, the 2017 roadmap's "Holochain Package Manager" and "DPKI" are the Compository and CeptrNet identity reborn, and a former Holochain developer traced the framework's microservice composability "to the receptor notion in Ceptr" ([HN, tidrel-tabpub, 2020](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22861001)). But no source code moved: a code search finds zero hits for "ceptr" in holochain-proto and, in the Rust Holochain, only the authors' "Ceptr, LLC" affiliation in the white-paper TeX ([holochain white paper tex](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/holochain/holochain/develop/docs/specs/src/holochain-white-paper-alpha.tex)). Brock was candid that the central idea could not follow: "One of the things [Ceptr] has is a self-describing protocol stack... Holochain doesn't have that and probably can't exactly have that" ([Crypto Radio, Oct 2018](http://cryptoradio.io/arthur-brock-holochain/)). Harris-Braun's July 2017 conversation framed Holochain as "fertilizing the field so that then when you come along with something like Ceptr people have the capacity to know how to interact with it" ([YouTube chXQ9ve9r7g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chXQ9ve9r7g)), and Brock in 2018 called Ceptr "shelved at the moment while we work on getting Holochain out there, because we think Holochain actually is ripe for adoption and Ceptr's not. We view... Holochain as a bit of an onramp to Ceptr" ([Crypto Radio, Oct 2018](http://cryptoradio.io/arthur-brock-holochain/)).

The documentary trail then shows Ceptr fading from the Holochain story: the Go README calls Holochain "a sub-project of Ceptr" (2017); the February 2018 white paper mentions Ceptr only as the authors' affiliation; the Holo Green Paper (December 2017, updated 2021) keeps a glossary entry; and the November 2024 "White Paper 2.0" and the 2024 "Players of Ludos" paper contain no occurrence of the word at all ([holochain.org/papers](https://www.holochain.org/papers)). Harris-Braun's October 2025 strategic update as Holochain Foundation executive director, covering Holo's Edge Node and the Unyt value-tracking infrastructure, does not mention Ceptr either ([blog.holochain.org, 2 Oct 2025](https://blog.holochain.org/finding-our-edge-a-strategic-update/)).

## Reception stayed inside the founders' network, and 2026 ports revive the C code

Ceptr never reached a mainstream technical audience. On Hacker News it was submitted exactly once as a story (30 November 2018, 4 points, 1 comment, the submitter's own), and almost all of the roughly 27 comments that mention it come from two or three enthusiast accounts promoting "Protocol Cooperativism" in 2019–2021 ([HN Algolia search](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22ceptr%22&tags=comment)). A Lambda the Ultimate thread on the MIT presentation exists but could not be retrieved. The one substantive external comparison is the 2020 Urbit analogy by a former Holochain developer, who argued category theory would be the right "theoretical backbone" for "an entire operating system made of composable 'atoms' like Ceptr" ([HN item 22861001](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22861001)); the largest HN Holochain thread (2022, 43 comments) is skeptical ("a webpage entirely made of buzzwords," "no reason to pay any more attention to this than any other cryptocurrency-related 'Grand Vision', or even something like Urbit") and does not mention Ceptr ([HN item 32604428](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32604428)). No peer-reviewed paper on Ceptr exists; Crossref returns only Holochain papers from 2020 onward, and the full text of a 2022 Discover Education paper on Holochain contains no occurrence of "Ceptr" or "MetaCurrency" ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-022-00013-y)). Long-form treatments are sympathetic explainers written during the Holo ICO era (Bitrates' three-part "Holochain Analysis" of November 2018, rhyzom's "Currencies of Social Coherence," a Korean Steemit reading of the Revelation that notes the source is "openly unfinished") ([bitrates.com](https://www.bitrates.com/news/p/holochain-analysis-1-the-philosophy-and-key-concepts-behind-holochain-and-ceptr)). Tellingly, the sharpest critiques on record are the ones the founders reported themselves: "RDF does that," "sounds like the blockchain," "friggin' crazy to be trying to change things at the level that we are," and Harris-Braun's acknowledgement of "rumors that Ceptr and Holochain are a scam... really just blockchain with components named differently," rebutted with "Holochain does NOT MANAGE CONSENSUS" ([hackmd.io](https://hackmd.io/qxC2LKHQTfaqDhZTP2TorQ)). There is no independent technical critique of the semantic-tree or Semtrex design anywhere in the located record, which is itself a finding: the design was never engaged seriously enough to be refuted.

As of October 2026 the public status is frozen-but-not-dead. ceptr.org is online (Jekyll, site last rebuilt 12 June 2024) but its homepage still reads "funded only by the sweat of our team (as of January 2017 -- this could change soon)" and its newest post is from June 2017; the "Ceptr Core" and "Pluggable Protocols" navigation links return 404 while the Holochain page is live ([ceptr.org](https://ceptr.org/)). Brock's personal site lists Ceptr under "Current projects" with an empty "Past Projects" section ([artbrock.com/projects](https://www.artbrock.com/projects/)), and Holo's 2023 press kit still bills him as founder of "Holochain, Ceptr, and Agile Learning Centers" ([press.holo.host](https://press.holo.host/media_kits/230328)). The `zippy/ceptr` README has never withdrawn its 2017 "alive and well... will return... soon" language.

The February 2026 activity is the first new engineering in a decade. On 22 February 2026 Harris-Braun committed "fix: compile and run was broken," a three-line change adding `-fcommon` to the Makefile for modern gcc and correcting a semantic-table lookup ([commit 3de2c89](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr/commit/3de2c897b8c802ada2a6db9b91aaaa496a0ff7e8)). The same day he created **`zippy/ceptr-js`**, "javascript implementation of semantic trees and semtrex," 4,486 lines of TypeScript in 18 files with 157 vitest cases and zero runtime dependencies, deliberately scoped to "two core ideas from the Ceptr project" and offering the only quick-start example in the whole ecosystem (define symbols, build a tree, match `/TASK/(TITLE,STATUS,.*)`, capture, embody) ([github.com/zippy/ceptr-js](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr-js)). On 23 February he pushed **`zippy/ceptr-rs`**, "full port of ceptr to rust": 26,105 lines across three crates (`ceptr-tree`, `ceptr-semtrex`, `ceptr-core`), sharing the original `base_defs` file with the C code, under a copyright line now reading "Copyright (C) 2013-2025, Ceptr LLC & The MetaCurrency Project" ([github.com/zippy/ceptr-rs](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr-rs)). The repository carries a `CLAUDE.md` whose goal is a "FULL PORT of the C codebase's functionality, behavior, and processing model. No short-cuts," instructing the agent to "run the C binary and compare" before marking features complete ([ceptr-rs CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr-rs/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)); together with "Pass 1–8" analysis documents, this is the signature of an AI-assisted porting workflow, though neither repository says so explicitly. The port's own eighth-pass assessment is the most honest status statement Ceptr has ever had: "400 tests passing," "individual instruction coverage: ~95%," but "System-level behavioral fidelity: LOW" and "The system has never been composed end-to-end using the ceptr processing model," with the Rust shell using "a synchronous function call" instead of signals; `stream` and `protocol` are listed as "stub, planned for later," and of ~120 `@todo` annotations in the C code about 65 are fixed, 23 unaddressed and 30 not applicable ([ceptr-rs PORT-ANALYSIS.md](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr-rs/blob/main/PORT-ANALYSIS.md); [TODO.md](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr-rs/blob/main/TODO.md)). Neither port is published to a package registry, no public statement from either founder explains the revival, and whether "Ceptr LLC" signals a product intent is unknown. The reasonable reading is that the ten-year-old C prototype was revived precisely because it is the only executable ground truth for the design, and that language-model tooling lowered the cost of the Go rewrite the founders floated in 2017 to the point where it finally happened, in Rust and TypeScript, nine years late.

## Conclusion

Ceptr's record inverts the usual relationship between a vision document and its implementation. The ambitious parts of the writing, CeptrNet, the Compository, membranes, holographic storage, scape algebra, were never coded, while the parts that were coded, a homoiconic semantic-tree substrate with a tree-automaton pattern language, late-bound executable protocols and a self-reducing VM, are a genuinely novel and internally consistent design that nobody outside the project has ever evaluated, because the project's only public success came from the one component that did not depend on them. Holochain inherited Ceptr's principles (agent-centricity, intrinsic data integrity, "trust your own node," forkable sovereignty) and its biological vocabulary, but it could not inherit the self-describing protocol stack, and Brock said so. That leaves the central Ceptr claim, that interoperability requires semantics at the lowest layer of storage and computation rather than at the API layer, untested in practice a decade after it was made.

The 2026 ports change the question from "will Ceptr return?" to "what is the C prototype worth as a specification?" By treating 17,800 lines of 2016 C as ground truth for Rust and TypeScript reimplementations, Harris-Braun has effectively declared the intra-host design finished enough to preserve, and the port's admission that the system "has never been composed end-to-end" marks exactly where the next real work lies: composing receptors, protocols and signals into a running whole, then confronting the inter-host problems that were handed to Holochain and never returned. Whether that happens, Ceptr already stands as an unusually complete case study in how a two-person research program can seed an ecosystem while the program itself goes unread, and the surviving code is the clearest place to start reading it.
