Respecting Equality: Complete Inference, Deterministic and Step-Optimal

Avik Jain

This is one construction on how equality behaves in cubical type theory: equality of types is equivalence — Voevodsky's univalence — and it computes. No prior familiarity with either is assumed.

From that one fact, each step forced by the last, the construction builds to a single result: complete inference — proving, computing, deciding, or answering any well-formed question — runs deterministically and in the fewest possible steps. The natural numbers, the complex numbers and the field equations, and the exact form of the Gödel and Turing limits are stops it passes through, not the destination. (0) is the fact, shown to be definitional; (1)–(4) are its forced consequences; (5) is the completion. Everything here is machine-checked in Agda (--safe, no postulates) and runs.

0. The one fact

  • A type is a set of values (a : A); a function f : A → B maps values to values. A pair type Σ (a : A) (P a) holds pairs (a , w) with w : P a.
  • Equality is a type: x = y is the type of proofs that x equals y; refl : x = x. A proof is a value you keep.
  • Contractible = isomorphic to the one-element type: one element, and any two elements equal.
  • Fact: Σ (y : A) (x = y) — the values y : A equal to x, each with its proof — is contractible: exactly one, (x , refl). This is the eliminator of = (path induction, J): the contraction sends every (y, p : x = y) to (x , refl), so a family on those pairs is fixed by its value there and transported outward — to act on every proof x = y it suffices to act at refl.
  • Equivalently, substitution: a proof p : x = y gives transport p : P x → P y and its inverse, with transport refl = id. A program that runs, reversible — lossless.
  • §1 is this fact with x replaced by a function's output.

1. Partition by output

  • f : A → B; the fibre over b is f⁻¹(b) = Σ (a) (f a = b) — each input with the proof it maps to b, kept.
  • A ≅ Σ (b : B) f⁻¹(b), by a ↦ (f a, a, refl). Nothing lost.
  • This lossless completion is unique — the completions of f form a contractible type (Ekatva, checked). A completion is a family T over B with A ≅ Σ (b) T(b) lying over f; unwound, that fixes at each output b a type equivalent to f⁻¹(b), and the space of types equivalent to a fixed one is contractible (univalence — exactly one, up to a path). So T is forced to be the fibre family: the decomposition is not a chosen encoding.
  • Every decomposition below is this: group by output, keep the fibre.

2. Isomorphism is substitution

  • A ≅ B (isomorphism): maps both ways with proofs they cancel — runnable in both directions.
  • Equivalently: every fibre f⁻¹(b) is contractible (§0) — the inverse g centers it at (g b, ·), and the cancellation laws contract every other (a, p : f a = b) onto that center.
  • Univalence: A ≅ B ⇒ A = B, and transport along it is f, executing. Isomorphic structures interchange by a program, losslessly.

3. The effect can be free; the steps are not

  • Two costs, and on reversible processes they diverge: the effect achieved, and the length — the number of primitive steps taken.
  • An additive effect-cost is a homomorphism ℓ : (net effects, ∘) → (ℕ, +): ℓ(id) = 0, ℓ(g ∘ h) = ℓ g + ℓ h.
  • On a group it vanishes:
$$ \ell(g) + \ell(g^{-1}) = \ell(e) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \ell(g) = 0 \qquad (\text{naturals are} \ge 0) $$
  • So a reversible net effect is free on this axis: transport and every isomorphism carry no additive cost — this is where Landauer's principle sits (§5). (Laghava, checked.)
  • Length is not that grading. Minimum length over a fixed set of primitive steps is only subadditive — strict under cancellation — and is positive on reversible operations: bringing cell n to the head is reversible with minimum length exactly n (§5). Reversible ≠ free in steps.
  • Both are relative to the observation: what is erased is measured against what the inference keeps — keep the fibre (§1) and nothing is erased — and the length charged is the steps the requested output forces, not those to reconstruct a whole trace.

4. The diagonal

  • e : A → (A → B) is onto: every h : A → B equals e a for some a.
  • Then every g : B → B has a fixed point:
$$ A \twoheadrightarrow (A \to B) \;\Longrightarrow\; \forall\, g : B \to B,\ \ \exists\, b,\ g(b) = b $$
  • h a := g(e a a); e onto gives h = e a₀; so:
$$ e(a_0)(a_0) \;=\; h(a_0) \;=\; g\big(e(a_0)(a_0)\big) $$
  • e a₀ a₀ is a fixed point of g.
  • Contrapositive: g with no fixed point ⇒ no A → (A → B) is onto.
  • Cantor. B = 2, g = ¬ (negation has no fixed point): no surjection A ↠ (A → 2) = 𝒫(A).
  • Gödel. B = provability values, g = negation: no provability predicate is point-surjective onto the sentences, and the missing fixed point is the sentence asserting its own unprovability.
  • Halting. B = 2, g = ¬ on the universal machine's diagonal: no machine decides its own halting.

One lemma; the walls are its instances.

5. Complete inference is deterministic and step-optimal

  • The correspondence (Curry–Howard–Lambek). A proposition is a type; a proof of it is a term; evaluating the term is normalizing it. Proving, deciding, computing, and answering a well-formed question are therefore one operation: normalize a term. (Curry 1934; Howard 1969; Lambek 1980.)
  • Cost is a local potential (Bellman). In the reduction graph — vertices terms, edges primitive steps of cost c(u→v) — a potential is any Φ with Φ(normal form)=0 and Φ(u) ≤ c(u→v)+Φ(v) on every edge; each such Φ lower-bounds every terminal path, and the geodesic distance is the greatest one, satisfying the Bellman optimality equation (Bellman 1957):
$$ \Phi(\text{normal form}) = 0, \qquad \Phi(u) = \min_{v}\, \big[\, c(u \to v) + \Phi(v)\,\big] $$
  • Two axes, from §3. A reduction step takes time whether or not it erases — length counts steps, and reversible steps cost length. Reversibility instead makes the effect-cost free: a step that keeps its fibre is a bijection, so its additive effect-cost is 0 (§3). Erasing k bits is the irreducible effect-cost — Landauer's principle: at least kT ln 2 per bit, reversible computation dissipating nothing (Landauer 1961; Bennett 1973, 1982).
  • Step-optimality: every computation attains the geodesic. The fewest primitive steps realizing the requested output is forced by the dependency geometry — each step is 1-Lipschitz with unit lookahead (SthairyaSutra, checked), so a k-step word cannot make an output depend on input it has not reached: a potential (above) lower-bounding every word, not one chosen strategy — and the native evolution attains it, lowering the potential by exactly one per step. The output is a complete proof-carrying witness — the answer with its certificate — whose size is forced by the requested type; in the kernel the route's length equals that size (cost = output size, AnswerIsProjectionAtOutputSize, checked — building sucⁿ lays down one successor per step, so n steps for an output of size n): the reduction constructs exactly the witness and nothing more — the definitional floor met with equality, no step discarded. Reading it off is a free projection (eval, a fold); the classical exponential lives only in the lossy eval compression (unary→succinct), never in the reduction. (Lévy 1978; Lamping 1990; Lafont 1990 are the prior, weaker form — optimal within a fixed system's own count.)
  • Why every reduction is the minimum, in general. Distinct redexes do not interfere: if a term steps two ways, either the two results coincide or each steps once more to a common term — the one-step diamond. So any available first step peels through any reduction to a normal form: at each stage the diamond either merges it or carries it one place forward, and at the normal form no step is left to carry, so the first step is absorbed exactly once and that reduction comes out one shorter. Induct on length — any two reductions of a term to the same normal form have equal length, so each is the minimum: the step-count is fixed by the endpoints, never the strategy, and the cost is as deterministic as the answer. This needs no term-specific hypothesis, only the diamond: for any step relation with it, Trace n s t → Trace m s t → Normal t → n ≤ m (normalization-is-geodesic, checked).
  • Complete, deterministic, terminating. Sharing every isomorphic subterm (folding all equalities) makes the normal form unique — confluence, Church–Rosser — and reached in finitely many forced steps; a non-terminating term denotes a well-defined infinite (coinductive) object, not a gap.
  • The price of an answer is the information the question erases. The effect-axis price is exactly the information a step irreversibly destroys — the non-contractible residue it collapses (§§1–3) — relative to the observation: keep the fibre and the step is free; force a collapse and you pay the bits erased, for the requested output, not a whole trace. Landauer's bound, as a theorem of the reduction. The length cost (the geodesic; the steps taken) is a separate axis, and no cardinality measures it (Search).
  • No outer layer. Evaluator, type-checker, and optimizer are themselves terms of the same language; a question about them is another term the same normalizer reduces. There is no meta-level, so no self-reference difficulty: any expressible system — this one included — is lawful construction and identity, operated on exactly as everything else is. Numbers

  • card : {finite sets} → ℕ. The fibre over n — the n-element sets — is connected: one isomorphism class, any two related by a relabelling, with automorphism group Sₙ. It is the classifying type BSₙ, not a point.
  • Relabellings of an n-set = the permutation group Sₙ, n! of them:
$$ \mathbb{N} = \{\text{finite sets}\}\,/\,\cong, \qquad \text{relabellings of an } n\text{-set} = S_n\ \ (n!\ \text{of them}) $$
  • The number is that fibre with Sₙ forgotten — Sₙ is exactly what “up to isomorphism” discards.
  • Addition = disjoint union (|A ⊔ B| = |A| + |B|), multiplication = product (|A × B| = |A|·|B|); on digit strings, carrying.
  • Divisibility orders ℕ into a distributive lattice: gcd = meet, lcm = join (BezoutIsGCD, LCMExists, checked), and by unique factorization ℕ is the product of one chain per prime (Bahuguna, checked).
  • That is the same identity as the Boolean algebra of logic (Search): gcd/lcm ↔ ∧/∨, coprime ↔ disjoint, CRT ℤ/mn ≅ ℤ/m × ℤ/n ↔ the product decomposition (CRTChain, checked), De Morgan on both. Number theory and logic are one distributive lattice — the far ends joined by pure identity.

Complex numbers and quantum dynamics — the reversible side of §3

  • σ(x, y) = (-y, x) — rotation by 90°, a length-preserving order-4 rotation; on x + iy it is × i. It is reversible — lossless, and free on §3's effect axis.
  • The complex numbers, the four phases {1, i, -1, -i}, and unitarity (length-preservation) are generated by this one reversible operation. Unitary evolution is exactly §3's free, reversible side; the physics that costs — measurement, erasure — is the irreversible side (Landauer, §5).
  • Riemann–Silberstein. With F = E + iB, Maxwell's two vacuum equations become one Schrödinger equation, generator × i:
$$ \partial_t E = \nabla\times B,\ \ \partial_t B = -\nabla\times E \;\Longleftrightarrow\; i\,\partial_t F = \nabla\times F $$
  • Classical electromagnetism and single-particle quantum mechanics are the same equation.
  • ‖F‖² = ‖E‖² + ‖B‖² is the conserved energy — length preserved, i.e. unitary.

Search

$$ \text{solutions of } F \;=\; F^{-1}(\text{true}) \;=\; \{\, w : F(w) = \text{true} \,\}, \qquad \#\text{solutions} = \sum_{T \subseteq C} (-1)^{|T|}\, 2^{\,n - r(T)} $$
  • F : assignments → {true, false}. Solutions S_F = F⁻¹(true); each clause violates on one codimension-3 subcube V_α, and Q_N = S_F ⊍ ⋃ V_α — deciding and solving are two observations of one partition. In the count below, r(T) is the number of coordinates the clauses in T jointly fix, so |⋂_{α∈T} V_α| = 2^{N−r(T)} (and 0 when two conflict); the alternating sign is forced — an assignment breaking exactly j clauses is counted with weight Σ_{i≤j} C(j,i)(−1)^i = (1−1)^j, which is 1 only at j = 0, so each solution survives once and the rest cancel.
  • Deciding = ‖S_F‖ (one bit: is S_F inhabited). Solving = S_F (a vertex with its membership proof). The truncation τ : S_F → ‖S_F‖ sends every witness to the decision, and its fibre fibτ ≅ S_F (§0) — so the one-bit decision discards exactly the which-witness fibre, with no left inverse once |S_F| > 1.
  • Decide = verify. Over the universal step, decide completes an input to (answer, witness) — by §1 that completion is the equivalence A ≅ Σ (b) f⁻¹(b), here decide(mc) = (step mc, (mc, refl)): the answer, paired with the input and the refl proof it maps there. verify reads the witness's pre-image back out; the round trip is refl. Deciding and verifying are thus the two directions of that one equivalence, unique by Ekatva (VerifyIsDecide, checked) — the decided witness carries its own acceptance as refl, nothing is searched between having the answer and having its certificate, and the find/check gap has no carrier over the lossless machine.
  • #SAT = Σ_{T compatible} (−1)^|T| 2^{N−r(T)} = Σ_r (−1)^r M_r — the alternating collapse of the clause-intersection (factorial-moment) tower M_r = Σ_{|T|=r} |⋂ V|; the scalar count is an extremely lossy projection (1,−1,1,…)·(M_r). UNSAT ⇺ it is 0; an exactly-3 UNSAT formula needs m ≥ 8 (from Σ_x ν(x) = m·2^{N−3} ≥ 2^N), sharp.
  • §5's cost is a geodesic potential, never a cardinality. Assignment, vertex, intersection, and fixed-order residual-width counts all collapse under factoring or coordinate change (disjoint triples: m clauses on disjoint variable-triples each kill 1 of 8 and leave 7, so #SAT = 7^m while the intersections number 2^m; inner-product: width 2ⁿ one order, ≤4 another). A lower bound is a Φ with Φ(t)=0 and Φ(u) ≤ w(u,v)+Φ(v) on every primitive edge; distance is the greatest such Φ. An exponential lower bound would be an exponential packing of nonfillable boundary distinctions with unit incidence — the construction fixes exactly what would count, and posits none for SAT.
