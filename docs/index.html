Respecting Equality: Complete Inference, Deterministic and Step-Optimal

Avik Jain

This is one construction on how equality behaves in cubical type theory: equality of types is equivalence — Voevodsky's univalence — and it computes. No prior familiarity with either is assumed.

From that one fact, each step forced by the last, the construction builds to a single result: complete inference — proving, computing, deciding, or answering any well-formed question — runs deterministically and in the fewest possible steps. The natural numbers, the complex numbers and the field equations, and the exact form of the Gödel and Turing limits are stops it passes through, not the destination. (0) is the fact, shown to be definitional; (1)–(4) are its forced consequences; (5) is the completion. Everything here is machine-checked in Agda (--safe, no postulates) and runs.

0. The one fact

  • A type is a set of values (a : A); a function f : A → B maps values to values. A pair type Σ (a : A) (P a) holds pairs (a , w) with w : P a.
  • Equality is a type: x = y is the type of proofs that x equals y; refl : x = x. A proof is a value you keep.
  • Contractible = isomorphic to the one-element type: one element, and any two elements equal.
  • Fact: Σ (y : A) (x = y) — the values y : A equal to x, each with its proof — is contractible: exactly one, (x , refl). This contractibility is path induction (J): since every (y, p : x = y) equals the center (x , refl), a family defined on such pairs is fixed by its single value at (x , refl) — transport along that equality carries it to every (y, p). To act on all of =, it suffices to act at refl.
  • Equivalently, substitution: a proof p : x = y gives transport p : P x → P y and its inverse, with transport refl = id. A program that runs, reversible — lossless.
  • §1 is this fact with x replaced by a function's output.

1. Partition by output

  • f : A → B; the fibre over b is f⁻¹(b) = Σ (a) (f a = b) — each input with the proof it maps to b, kept.
  • A ≅ Σ (b : B) f⁻¹(b), by a ↦ (f a, a, refl). Nothing lost.
  • This lossless completion is unique — the completions of f form a contractible type (Ekatva, checked), so the decomposition is forced, not a chosen encoding.
  • Every decomposition below is this: group by output, keep the fibre.

2. Isomorphism is substitution

  • A ≅ B (isomorphism): maps both ways with proofs they cancel — runnable in both directions.
  • Equivalently, every fibre f⁻¹(b) is contractible: an inverse g puts a center (g b, ·) in the fibre, and the cancellation laws contract every other (a, p : f a = b) onto it — the §0 singleton shape. (Matching the two directions coherently is the standard identification of “has a two-sided inverse” with “is an equivalence.”)
  • Univalence: A ≅ B ⇒ A = B, and transport along it is f, executing. Isomorphic structures interchange by a program, losslessly.

3. The effect can be free; the steps are not

  • Two costs, and on reversible processes they diverge: the effect achieved, and the length — the number of primitive steps taken.
  • An additive effect-cost is a homomorphism ℓ : (net effects, ∘) → (ℕ, +): ℓ(id) = 0, ℓ(g ∘ h) = ℓ g + ℓ h.
  • On a group it vanishes:
$$ \ell(g) + \ell(g^{-1}) = \ell(e) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \ell(g) = 0 \qquad (\text{naturals are} \ge 0) $$
  • So a reversible net effect is free on this axis: transport and every isomorphism carry no additive cost — this is where Landauer's principle sits (§5). (Laghava, checked.)
  • Length is not that grading. Minimum length over a fixed set of primitive steps is only subadditive — strict under cancellation — and is positive on reversible operations: bringing cell n to the head is reversible with minimum length exactly n (§5). Reversible ≠ free in steps.
  • Both are relative to the observation: what is erased is measured against what the inference keeps — keep the fibre (§1) and nothing is erased — and the length charged is the steps the requested output forces, not those to reconstruct a whole trace.

4. The diagonal

  • e : A → (A → B) is onto: every h : A → B equals e a for some a.
  • Then every g : B → B has a fixed point:
$$ A \twoheadrightarrow (A \to B) \;\Longrightarrow\; \forall\, g : B \to B,\ \ \exists\, b,\ g(b) = b $$
  • h a := g(e a a); e onto gives h = e a₀; so:
$$ e(a_0)(a_0) \;=\; h(a_0) \;=\; g\big(e(a_0)(a_0)\big) $$
  • e a₀ a₀ is a fixed point of g.
  • Contrapositive: g with no fixed point ⇒ no A → (A → B) is onto.
  • Cantor. B = 2, g = ¬ (negation has no fixed point): no surjection A ↠ (A → 2) = 𝒫(A).
  • Gödel. B = provability values, g = negation: no provability predicate is point-surjective onto the sentences, and the missing fixed point is the sentence asserting its own unprovability.
  • Halting. B = 2, g = ¬ on the universal machine's diagonal: no machine decides its own halting.

One lemma; the walls are its instances. Turning each instance into the named classical theorem needs its own bridge — arithmetization and the diagonal lemma for Gödel, a universal machine for Halting — cited, not built here; what is built here is the single diagonal that forces all of them.

5. Complete inference is deterministic and step-optimal

  • The correspondence (Curry–Howard–Lambek). A proposition is a type; a proof of it is a term; evaluating the term is normalizing it. For a term that normalizes, proving, deciding, computing, and answering the question it poses are therefore one operation — normalize the term; the walls of §4 are exactly the terms with no normal form to reach, so no single total procedure decides every proposition. (Curry 1934; Howard 1969; Lambek 1980.)
  • Cost is a local potential (Bellman). In the reduction graph — vertices terms, edges primitive steps of cost c(u→v) — a potential is any Φ with Φ(normal form)=0 and Φ(u) ≤ c(u→v)+Φ(v) on every edge; each such Φ lower-bounds every terminal path, and the geodesic distance is the greatest one, satisfying the Bellman optimality equation (Bellman 1957):
$$ \Phi(\text{normal form}) = 0, \qquad \Phi(u) = \min_{v}\, \big[\, c(u \to v) + \Phi(v)\,\big] $$
  • Two axes, from §3. A reduction step takes time whether or not it erases — length counts steps, and reversible steps cost length. Reversibility instead makes the effect-cost free: a step that keeps its fibre is a bijection, so its additive effect-cost is 0 (§3). Erasing k bits is the irreducible effect-cost — Landauer's principle: at least kT ln 2 per bit, reversible computation dissipating nothing (Landauer 1961; Bennett 1973, 1982).
  • Step-optimality, built here — the cost is deterministic. Model complete inference as reduction to a normal form. Local steps satisfy the one-step diamond: if a term reduces in one step two ways, either the two results coincide or each reduces in one further step to a common term — distinct redexes do not interfere, the defining local confluence of an interaction net. From the diamond alone, peel: given any available first step and any reduction of the same term to a normal form, push that step through the reduction one stage at a time; the diamond either merges it (results coincided) or carries it forward one place, and at the normal form no forward branch can survive — so the first step is absorbed exactly once, and the reduction to that normal form comes out exactly one step shorter. Induct on length: any two reductions of a term to the same normal form have equal length. So the step-count is fixed by the endpoints, never by the strategy — every complete reduction is at once the minimum: a geodesic. Determinism is not only of the answer (confluence) but of its cost. (Built in general as normalization-is-geodesic: for any step relation with the diamond, Trace n s t → Trace m s t → Normal t → n ≤ m — no term-specific hypothesis.)
  • The geodesic is not zero: it is the depth the answer reads. Equal length does not yet say the length is large; a separating input shows it is exactly the dependency depth. Take the local action where a step's output at a cell depends only on that cell and its neighbour — unit lookahead; composing k steps, the head then depends only on the input within distance k (induct: each step widens reach by one). Now take two inputs agreeing up to depth n and differing only at n: a word of length k < n gives the same head on both, so it cannot realize an observation whose value differs there — while the explicit word carrying depth n to the head takes exactly n steps. So the minimum is exactly n, the depth the answer depends on, and the bound ranges over every word of the alphabet, not one strategy (SthairyaSutra's prefix locality, BringGeodesic's separating pair). Reversible and yet positive distance: semantic invertibility and execution distance are different structures.
  • The route is the size of the answer. A completed reduction is the answer carrying its own certificate — a proof-carrying term whose size is fixed by the requested type. In the kernel that builds sucⁿ, each step lays down one successor, so the route has length n and the output has size n: route length equals output size, by the one count — the reduction constructs exactly the witness and nothing more, the definitional floor met with equality, no step discarded. Reading the finished term off is a fold that copies it and adds no step; that is why eval is refl. So no super-linear cost lives in the reduction; the classical exponential appears only when that unary witness is re-measured under a succinct (binary) encoding — a fact about the lossy remeasurement, not the route. (The length-equals-size equation is what AnswerIsProjectionAtOutputSize labels; Lévy 1978, Lamping 1990, Lafont 1990 are the prior, weaker form — optimal within a fixed system's own count.)
  • Complete, deterministic, terminating. Sharing every isomorphic subterm (folding all equalities) makes the normal form unique — confluence, Church–Rosser — and reached in finitely many forced steps; a non-terminating term denotes a well-defined infinite (coinductive) object, not a gap.
  • The price of an answer is the information the question erases. The effect-axis price is exactly the information a step irreversibly destroys — the non-contractible residue it collapses (§§1–3) — relative to the observation: keep the fibre and the step is free; force a collapse and you pay the bits erased, for the requested output, not a whole trace. That the price is exactly the erased residue — zero iff the step is reversible — is a theorem of the reduction (§3); identifying one erased bit with kT ln 2 of dissipated heat is Landauer's physical principle, imported. The length cost (the geodesic; the steps taken) is a separate axis, and no cardinality measures it (Search).
  • No outer layer. Evaluator, type-checker, and optimizer are themselves terms of the same language; a question about them is another term the same normalizer reduces. There is no meta-level, so no self-reference difficulty: any expressible system — this one included — is lawful construction and identity, operated on exactly as everything else is. (Checked in cubical Agda, run on the HVM interaction-net runtime.)

Numbers

  • card : {finite sets} → ℕ. The fibre over n — the n-element sets — is connected: one isomorphism class, any two related by a relabelling, with automorphism group Sₙ. It is the classifying type BSₙ, not a point.
  • Relabellings of an n-set = the permutation group Sₙ, n! of them:
$$ \mathbb{N} = \{\text{finite sets}\}\,/\,\cong, \qquad \text{relabellings of an } n\text{-set} = S_n\ \ (n!\ \text{of them}) $$
  • The number is that fibre with Sₙ forgotten — Sₙ is exactly what “up to isomorphism” discards.
  • Addition = disjoint union (|A ⊔ B| = |A| + |B|), multiplication = product (|A × B| = |A|·|B|); on digit strings, carrying.
  • Divisibility orders ℕ into a distributive lattice: gcd = meet, lcm = join (BezoutIsGCD, LCMExists, checked), and by unique factorization ℕ is the product of one chain per prime (Bahuguna, checked).
  • That is the same distributive-lattice structure as the algebra of logic (Search): gcd/lcm ↔ ∧/∨, coprime ↔ disjoint, CRT ℤ/mn ≅ ℤ/m × ℤ/n ↔ the product decomposition (CRTChain, checked), De Morgan on both. What is literally shared is the distributive-lattice structure — gcd/lcm and ∧/∨ obeying the same laws. They part at complementation: logic is Boolean (every proposition has a negation), while a divisor of n has a complement n/d that meets it in 1 and joins it to n exactly when n is squarefree — otherwise no complement exists. So the identity holds at the distributive-lattice level; Boolean structure is an extra layer logic carries throughout and divisibility carries only on the squarefree n.

Complex numbers and quantum dynamics — the reversible side of §3

  • σ(x, y) = (-y, x) — rotation by 90°, a length-preserving order-4 rotation; on x + iy it is × i. It is reversible — lossless, and free on §3's effect axis.
  • Adjoining this one reversible operation to the reals presents the complex numbers, ℂ = ℝ[i]/(i²+1), with × i the operation itself; its powers are the four phases {1, i, -1, -i}, and its being an isometry is unitarity (length-preservation). Unitary evolution is exactly §3's free, reversible side; the physics that costs — measurement, erasure — is the irreversible side (Landauer, §5).
  • Riemann–Silberstein. With F = E + iB, Maxwell's two vacuum curl equations combine into one complex first-order equation with generator × i — the free-photon wave equation (a Weyl-type form, not the second-order Schrödinger equation):
$$ \partial_t E = \nabla\times B,\ \ \partial_t B = -\nabla\times E \;\Longleftrightarrow\; i\,\partial_t F = \nabla\times F $$
  • The vacuum electromagnetic field and the free single photon's wavefunction obey this identical complex-linear evolution — that shared equational form is the claim here, not an identification of the full theories (measurement and the Born rule are the irreversible side, §5).
  • ‖F‖² = ‖E‖² + ‖B‖² is the conserved energy — length preserved, i.e. unitary.

Search

$$ \text{solutions of } F \;=\; F^{-1}(\text{true}) \;=\; \{\, w : F(w) = \text{true} \,\}, \qquad \#\text{solutions} = \sum_{T \subseteq C} (-1)^{|T|}\, 2^{\,n - r(T)} $$
  • F : assignments → {true, false}. Solutions S_F = F⁻¹(true); for 3-SAT each clause fixes its 3 literals, so it violates on one codimension-3 subcube V_α, and Q_N = S_F ⊍ ⋃ V_α — deciding and solving are two observations of one partition. The headline count is inclusion–exclusion over the V_α: for T ⊆ C, r(T) is the number of coordinates the clauses in T jointly fix, so |⋂_{α∈T} V_α| = 2^{n−r(T)} when they are compatible and 0 when two of them conflict. The alternating sign is forced, not invoked: an assignment violating exactly j clauses is counted by each subset of the clauses it violates, with total weight Σ_{i≤j} C(j,i)(−1)^i = (1−1)^j — which is 1 at j = 0 and 0 above it, so the sum keeps each solution once and cancels every non-solution.
  • Deciding = ‖S_F‖ (one bit: is S_F inhabited). Solving = S_F (a vertex with its membership proof). The truncation τ : S_F → ‖S_F‖ sends every witness to the decision, and its fibre fibτ ≅ S_F (§0) — so the one-bit decision discards exactly the which-witness fibre, with no left inverse once |S_F| > 1.
  • Decide = verify. Deciding (produce the answer) and verifying (check a candidate) are the two projections of one equivalence — the unique lossless completion of the step (VerifyIsDecide, Ekatva, checked) — and the decided witness carries its own acceptance proof as refl. Nothing is searched between having the answer and having its certificate. The find/check gap here is the information the lossy succinct measure discards, not a step-count separation — this construction posits none (VerifyIsDecide is explicit on that).
  • #SAT = Σ_{T compatible} (−1)^|T| 2^{N−r(T)} = Σ_r (−1)^r M_r — the alternating collapse of the clause-intersection (factorial-moment) tower M_r = Σ_{|T|=r} |⋂ V|; the scalar count is an extremely lossy projection (1,−1,1,…)·(M_r). UNSAT ⇺ it is 0; an exactly-3 UNSAT formula needs m ≥ 8 (from Σ_x ν(x) = m·2^{N−3} ≥ 2^N), sharp.
  • §5's cost is a geodesic potential, never a cardinality. Assignment, vertex, intersection, and fixed-order residual-width counts all collapse under factoring or coordinate change: put m clauses on disjoint variable-triples, each forbidding 1 of its triple's 8 assignments and leaving 7; across the independent triples #SAT = 7^m, while the compatible clause-subsets number 2^m — the same instance, two exponentials in different bases. (The inner-product function is the standard branching-program example where a fixed variable order gives residual width 2ⁿ and another order ≤ 4.) A lower bound is a Φ with Φ(t)=0 and Φ(u) ≤ w(u,v)+Φ(v) on every primitive edge; distance is the greatest such Φ. An exponential lower bound would be an exponential packing of nonfillable boundary distinctions with unit incidence — the construction fixes exactly what would count, and posits none for SAT.
