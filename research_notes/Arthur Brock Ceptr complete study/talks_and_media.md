# Arthur Brock / Eric Harris-Braun Ceptr talks, videos, podcasts, decks and interviews (annotated catalogue)

Scope note: Ceptr was publicly "unveiled" only in Sept 2014 and was effectively paused in Jan 2017 when Holochain was split off, so the dense core of Ceptr-specific spoken material is 2014–2017. Everything after that mentions Ceptr only as the origin/long-term vision of Holochain. Dates below are either stated on the source page or taken from YouTube upload metadata (retrieved with yt-dlp on 2026-10-08); where recording date and upload date differ, both are given. Durations come from YouTube metadata. Transcript-derived summaries rely on YouTube auto-captions, which mis-hear "Ceptr" as "scepter/sceptre/septer/sector"; quotes have been normalized to "Ceptr" and are otherwise verbatim.

## Key Question 1: What is the full list of Ceptr-related recorded talks and decks with dates and URLs?

### Takeaway
There are roughly a dozen primary Ceptr-era recordings/decks (2014–2016), all self-published by the MetaCurrency Project rather than by conference organizers, plus a second cluster of 2017 "Ceptr Salon"/residency videos documenting the hand-off to Holochain and a third cluster of 2018 podcast/hackathon appearances in which Ceptr is described retrospectively. No conference-circuit Ceptr talk (IIW, Open Source Bridge, Bioneers, Burning Man, DWeb Summit, Bitcoin conferences) could be found; the only external venues were an MIT webinar series, a Brussels Global Brain Institute workshop (presented by Jean-François Noubel, not Brock), Impact Hub Oakland, IFTF Palo Alto (unrecorded) and the Rebooting Web of Trust workshop (paper only).

**Item-by-item catalogue — Ceptr era (2014–2016)**

1. **"What's been happening with the MetaCurrency project?" — Hangout On Air, 15 Sept 2014** (speakers: Arthur Brock, Eric Harris-Braun; venue: Google+ Hangout On Air; recording URL: not located). Brock's 16 Sept 2014 post "Coming out of the MetaCurrency Closet" says the project had spent "several years building Ceptr with little public explanation", that the team "held a livestreamed Hangout On Air to introduce the project, which had technical glitches", and links the Google+ event (https://plus.google.com/u/0/events/cccsptirbe75qk8aucg7p6eo5ic) — [ceptr.org 2014-09-16](https://ceptr.org/blog/2014-09-16-hello-world). The companion deck is the Prezi "MetaCurrency Update & Ceptr Introduction" by Arthur Brock, which references the "Hangout On Air" of September 15, 2014 (page last updated Jan 24, 2016). Content: MetaCurrency framed as "An Operating System for Spaceship Earth" with "Holoptinetworked sensors & feedback"; Ceptr built from "receptors," lightweight VMs that "run processes, hold state and organize data, and can contain other receptors," running on "servers, routers, computers, phones, and plug computers"; components named: CeptrNet protocol, VM host, edge receptors, distributed data engine, TCP-socket sync between instances; StreamScapes as "a receptor-based tool for managing communication data and channels" (RSS, email, Twitter, IM, blogs, Skype, CRM); group-process tools (workflows, voting, proxies, "logarithmic proxies"); slogans "Everything should just work together!" and "Keep Building on Itself"; sister projects Emerging Leader Labs and Agile Learning Centers — [Prezi hhwgksbmq7rf](https://prezi.com/hhwgksbmq7rf/metacurrency-update--ceptr-introduction)

2. **"Just a Spoonful of Ceptr" — dress-rehearsal Hangout On Air, 1 April 2015** (Brock + Harris-Braun; Marc Clifton failed to dial in; Ferananda Ibarra ran the stream; YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE, 1:24:56, uploaded 2015-04-01, 382 views). Description: "today (April 1st) we unveil them first to you, our friends… DO expect to see: semantic trees, fractal receptors, semtrex as a general semantic pattern matching system, a preview of our distributed network technology, followed by Q&A" and "Do NOT expect a full-blown currency system" — [YouTube L9YqE4AW2sE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE). Transcript points: Harris-Braun opens "this is the dry run of a webinar that we're going to be hosting tomorrow with MIT KIT"; audience included Christopher Allen (named in chat) and Noah Thorp (asks how Ceptr compares to XDI); Brock: "we built Ceptr because… the current efforts like blockchain and APIs and RDF were falling far short"; receptors "sync with their other instances… uses kind of a holographic storage model a little bit like a distributed hashtable… provides distributed Byzantine fault tolerance"; compository: "imagine if the internet came with a built-in GitHub but it's not centralized"; on DAOs: "akin to… Ethereum distributed autonomous organizations… we encode them in a receptor… we can each take a copy of that receptor with us"; on Bitcoin: "they start with a distributed ledger and build on top of that; we kind of start the other way around with distributed process, distributed protocols"; "the Ceptr approach is to allow for multiple realities that can merge or fail to merge… that's called forking"; time/"when is now" acknowledged as "an open problem for us… looked into NTP"; versioning: "the very first branch point from its root identifier is actually the version"; motivation example: a PayPal transaction "between the two of us… we have to go through like nine different centralized steps"; openness "was basically about unenclosability"; on XDI: "XDI has gotten a lot of things right and has been kind of undervalued… link contracts"; closing: "to have a friendly audience who would be willing to watch us fail" — [YouTube L9YqE4AW2sE auto-transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)

6. **"Distributed Receptor-Based Computing" — position paper for Rebooting the Web of Trust I, San Francisco, 3–4 Nov 2015** (Arthur Brock; written, not recorded). Claims Ceptr "is a rebuild of much of the computing stack optimized for decentralized sense-making, computation and composability"; lists what is working ("lightweight VMs ('receptors'), configurable Byzantine fault tolerance with signed chains, semantic memory and stacks, a semantic tree regex engine, a built-in code repository… persistent 'listeners'"); "Ceptr protocols are not textual descriptions for human protocol developers, but pluggable semantic code"; identity: "Addresses are not temporary, reassigned, or segmented for routing," progressive trust via "vouching or triangulation," keys in a sharded DHT, capability-token disclosure — [ceptr.org 2015-11-03](https://ceptr.org/blog/2015-11-03-distributed-receptor-based-computing)

## Key Question 2: How did Brock pitch Ceptr to different audiences (currency people, developers, blockchain audiences)?

### Takeaway
To developers he led with architecture (receptors, semantic trees, Semtrex, pluggable protocols, compository) and positioned Ceptr as "way beyond blockchain, APIs or RDF"; to currency/new-economy audiences he led with "currencies are not money" (college-degree, couchsurfing examples) and presented Ceptr as "the interoperable grammatic space" needed for currency constellations; to blockchain audiences (2017–18) he reframed the whole thing as agent-centric vs data-centric, with Ceptr as the longer-term "self-describing protocols" vision behind Holochain.

### Cited Findings
- Developer pitch (MIT KIT, 2015): "Ceptr is really the underlying low-level protocols for how to structure data, organize processing and communicate as well as share code"; "we're building it in C for speed and portability… potentially being embedded in things like wi-fi routers"; the target properties were "distributed and decentralized, interoperable and resilient… composable… semantic" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Developer pitch, answering "what's been your motivation" (a language-design question): "this emerged out of work that we were doing in the alternative currency space where we wanted complete decentralization and distribution and resilience"; "what has been more our inspiration… has been actually modeling this as much as possible off of nature, of physics and biology" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Clifton's developer framing (same webinar): "applications are incredibly monolithic… what I want to be able to do is put in my own visualizations, wire up the information and the data flow in my own way" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Developer recruiting ask (2015): the "dream candidate" to build BFT "would be able to program in C… and have some experience with… Paxos or another Byzantine fault tolerance tools"; "we're looking for… other people to do pair programming" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Currency-people pitch (MIT KIT Q&A, when asked why the world shows little interest in creating its own money): "we're not using the same word as money… think of it like a current… the ability to see flows"; "a college degree is a reputation currency… credits are a unit of account currency… grades are performance metric currency"; "couchsurfing… millions of bed nights… through reputation currencies"; "all of those data packages live in large silos… Ceptr is our answer to that, it's the interoperable grammatic space" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Currency-people pitch (April 1 dry run): asked what Ceptr "has empowered currency constellations," Brock answers "the fundamental composability of everything talking to each other is what enables currency constellations… one currency to listen for some dimension inside of another currency" — [YouTube L9YqE4AW2sE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)
- Currency-people pitch (Noubel, Brussels 2015): Ceptr framed as "expressive capacities" for "a language of wealth" beyond money's "one dimensional language," with a mutual-credit-plus-reputation worked example — [YouTube muWnEF7JFjA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=muWnEF7JFjA)
- The webinar dry run opened with a poll asking viewers to rate "how techy you are, how geeky you feel yourself to be… because this whole webinar is fairly technical" — [YouTube L9YqE4AW2sE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)
- Social-change/general audience pitch (Tammy Lea Meyer, June 2017): "we have kind of a grand vision about building the tools for the next economy… the post-industrial age economy and that includes post monetary"; Ceptr "is short for receptor because a big part of the design… came out of a breakthrough about receptive capacities, a way to have these computing systems not be brittle"; "it's kind of like rebuilding our whole computing stack and internet" — [YouTube 3-5ph2ID3iA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3-5ph2ID3iA)
- Blockchain/crypto audience pitch (CryptoTickerDE, Feb 2018): on MetaCurrency's founding — "if you just make another centralized currency system but you change who gets to issue the money… you're just gonna turn the world over from the bankers to the geeks"; the four early principles were "open rules, open protocols, open transport and open identity," later collapsed into "enclosability"; sound waves vs a written note as unenclosable vs enclosable carriers; "that's really the point of blockchain that most people have missed… we've created an unenclosable data space" — [YouTube x8ta-561BSE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8ta-561BSE)
- Blockchain audience (same): Ceptr described as "rebuilding the computing stack for the next leap in collective intelligence that we need as a species"; asked whether Holochain apps would coexist with Facebook/Twitter: "in the long run I think Ceptr will win with its self-describing protocols because of the information fragmentation problem" — [YouTube x8ta-561BSE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8ta-561BSE)
- Blockchain audience (same): "I would go so far as to be extremely heretical… we are headed… to a post monetary economy"; "proof of work and proof of stake are both centralization algorithms"; "94% of Bitcoin being under the control of just five mining pools"; "it would be hard to make a more faithful reproduction of all of the problems of centralized money than Bitcoin" — [YouTube x8ta-561BSE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8ta-561BSE)
- Hackathon-developer pitch (Denver, July 2018): "we started building something called Ceptr which we're not going to talk about much because it's crazy and weird and requires you to bend your brains in painful ways"; "Ceptr… is the Latin to catch… to receive is to catch again"; blockchain "is currently using 0.5% of the planet's energy for mining" (Brock's claim, unsourced in the talk); "we specifically spun off Holochain to plot an intercept course with blockchain to try to Aikido all of that enthusiasm, energy, resources, attention on to a more sustainable track" — [YouTube wBlwQpcV95M transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wBlwQpcV95M)

- Identity-community pitch (RWOT paper, Nov 2015): single architecture addresses "web of trust, blockchain, semantic web, federated identity, and mesh networking"; concedes "the different approach creates adoption barriers" — [ceptr.org 2015-11-03](https://ceptr.org/blog/2015-11-03-distributed-receptor-based-computing)

### Inferences
- The pitch moved from "we need new infrastructure for currencies" (2014–15, audiences of friends, MIT, currency practitioners) to "we are the alternative to blockchain" (2017–18) as the crypto boom supplied the audience; Ceptr itself was increasingly described as the deferred, "brain-bending" long-term vision.
- The consistent rhetorical devices across audiences are biological analogies (cells, membranes, hormones/receptors, DNA/genotype-phenotype), the college-degree currency example, and the "unenclosability" criterion.

### Gaps
- No recording of Brock presenting Ceptr to a pure cryptocurrency-conference audience in 2014–2016 was found; the earliest blockchain-audience material is the 2018 interviews.

## Key Question 3: What specific examples/demos were shown?

### Takeaway
The recorded demos are: Marc Clifton's HOPE "APOD" receptor demo, the "home location" semantic-tree video, the "mailing label" run-tree video, a Semtrex HTTP-path match, an HTTP protocol definition as expectations, and (in the Tech Overview deck) an email/SMTP receptor; the "WordPress blog in receptors," "clock receptors" and "agreement engines" demos named in the brief do not appear in any recording, deck or transcript found.

### Cited Findings
- HOPE demo (MIT KIT webinar, pre-recorded video by Marc Clifton): "specialized and general-purpose autonomous computing units called receptors… membranes define bi-directional permeability within receptor groups"; the demo scrapes "NASA's astronomy post of the day (APOD) website," feeds a date ("valentine's day of 2014") from a "signal creator receptor," adds a "webpage scraper," wraps it in a membrane "permeable only to the url as a root semantic type" to stop a feedback loop, then adds an image viewer, a description display and a web page viewer that runs "a google search on the title" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA); Brock's gloss: HOPE "isn't actually built on Ceptr… he has taken these patterns that we're using" — [YouTube L9YqE4AW2sE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)
- Semantic-tree data example: "a home location which is a semantically identified latitude and longitude… a lat long is a composition of a latitude and a longitude each of which are structurally a floating point number… a mantissa and an exponent… bits"; "you can't instantiate meaningless data… you can't store an int, you can store an age or a shoe size"; Brock notes "not all of the data gets stored down to the bit level… that would be inefficient" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Run-tree process example (Harris-Braun): "taking a user profile and building a mailing label out of it… a set of nested concatenate strings"; parameters referenced by "tree path"; rendered with "the d3 visualization library… like a hex dump of the running code"; "a semantic tree can act as a semantic stack at the same time" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Semtrex example: "an http request as a semantic tree… the percentage sign means walk… find any match that matches the request path segments"; "it becomes a generalized parser that can parse any semantic structure or any protocol" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Expectations: "very similar to rules in Prolog… first you identify what carrier you're listening on and then what pattern… and if you get a match… this action gets fired"; listeners as planted expectations enabling "cascades" in "a multi-layered protocol" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Pluggable protocol example: "the HTTP 1.1 specification is almost 300 pages long of text… it's not something that runs"; in Ceptr "http is pretty simple, there's just server and client and there's only two types of messages… request and response," with the action handing the file and query string to "some sort of document handler"; "SMTP is actually a much more complex specification in Ceptr"; adapters: "you can also make receptors that are adapters" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Compository/versioning example: "you branch from an identifier down to versions in sub-branches so that when you pop a new version of a receptor the identity of it doesn't change" and an "immune system… based on the reputation of both authors and reviewers" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- IPv6 analogy: "the conversion from IPv4 to IPv6, it's going to be like a 30-year span… we see the need for those types of changes to happen faster" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- Cryptocurrency-receptor example (MIT KIT Q&A): "you're running a receptor of a cryptocurrency on your machine, you can trust your receptor so you can make an interaction and make a commit locally and then the data synchronization is the part that negotiates the consensus"; "I try to do a transaction with Eric, Eric declines the transaction and I'm just going to avoid my transaction when I try to synchronize" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)

## Key Question 4: What was the "Beyond Blockchain"/"Blockchain is not the answer" framing, and how did Ceptr appear in talks that predate Holochain?

### Takeaway
Before Holochain (2015), Ceptr's anti-blockchain framing was architectural: Bitcoin "starts with a distributed ledger," Ceptr starts with distributed computation and builds "intrinsic data integrity" on top, commits locally and synchronizes afterwards, and treats forking as a feature ("multiple realities"). From 2017 the framing hardened into "agent-centric vs data-centric," the "two core fallacies" (data exists, time exists), and the claim that proof-of-work/stake are centralizing; the literal phrase "Beyond Blockchain" appears as the Plutopia interview title (2018) and in Schutte's reference to a "beyond blockchain article."

### Cited Findings
- 2015 slide claim: "Way beyond block-chain, APIs or RDF!" — [Prezi y_7109dt0gzj](https://prezi.com/y_7109dt0gzj/just-a-spoonful-of-ceptr/)
- 2015 (MIT KIT): BFT "built in at this really low level… allows fully distributed applications sort of akin to blockchain crypto type solutions"; "our implementation of byzantine fault tolerance is different than… a blockchain ledger or Paxos… where basically you can't commit something… until you've reached agreement"; "we actually have multiple states of reality that are always trying to re-consensualize and sometimes they can't reconcile… that's okay because that's actually designing for forking" — [YouTube 3Db-8lD1lNA transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Db-8lD1lNA)
- 2015 (dry run): "the big differences between the Bitcoin approach and the Ceptr approach is… they start with a distributed ledger and build on top of that; we… start the other way around with distributed process… then we build intrinsic data integrity and ledgers… on top"; "a kind of inversion of approach which I think actually will get us computation in this distributed fashion much more efficiently than… mining"; "the Ethereum people are really doing some interesting things… it seems to us like a flip way of doing it"; Bitcoin is "trying to accomplish [unenclosability] except for the 51% problem" — [YouTube L9YqE4AW2sE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)
- 2015 (dry run): the compository is "in a sense a ledger of all of the compositions"; "we're taking something that looks much more like Ethereum and trying to put that at the lower level where the computational units are what's spread about… Bitcoin just has one thing that gets stored" — [YouTube L9YqE4AW2sE transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9YqE4AW2sE)

## Key Question 5: Which talks explain the transition from Ceptr to Holochain?

### Takeaway
The transition is explained most directly in Brock's June 2017 Tammy Lea Meyer interview ("we broke out the Holochain as a separate sub project from Ceptr basically at the beginning of January [2017]"), the July 2017 "Why are we building Holochain" conversation (Holochain as "a stepping stone… towards Ceptr"), and the 2018 Denver/Plutopia/Crypto Radio appearances ("we took one little part of Ceptr… multi-instance receptors… and made a standalone version to compete with blockchain"; Ceptr "shelved at the moment").

### Cited Findings
- Jan 2017 blog: "Restructuring Ceptr Into Sub-Projects" (Eric Harris-Braun, 17 Jan 2017) — [Ceptr Blog index](https://ceptr.org/blog/)
- GitHub README: "We pulled out a small part of Ceptr (synchronization for multi-instance receptors) and are going head-to-head with blockchain… That portion of Ceptr is called Holochain"; the rest of Ceptr "is paused, not abandoned," with a possible rewrite in Go — [github.com/zippy/ceptr via search excerpt](https://github.com/zippy/ceptr)

### Inferences
- Across all 2017–2018 accounts the story is consistent: Holochain = Ceptr's multi-instance-receptor synchronization/data-integrity layer, stripped of semantic trees, Semtrex and pluggable protocols, rewritten in Go, and launched as an "intercept course" with blockchain; Ceptr proper has been dormant since January 2017.
- The 2017 videos show the shift from Brock-and-Harris-Braun two-handers to a wider cast (Schutte, Holtz, Luck, residents), reflecting the "organizational phase shift" Brock blogged about in June 2017.
